Of all the 830-plus Gamines in the overview sheet, this is the car with the most recorded history. Besides the complete register row and public RDW data, there are two detailed eyewitness accounts on vignalegamine.net: The Flying Vignale (the purchase in Sydney and the transport by plane, 1991–1994) and Route des Vosges (the rally in the French Vosges, 1997). The timeline below runs from first admission in 1968 to today.

Timeline

From first registration (30 June 1968) to today (31 August 2026) — 58 years.

  1. 30 June 1968

    First admission. The Gamine is a right-hand-drive version and was originally sold in England. The year 1968 is certain; day and month are an RDW placeholder because they were not known at import. Source: RDW open data; overview sheet

  2. 1970s / 80s

    An earlier owner brings the car from England to Australia. There Carole Grace buys it, living in Huntershill, one of the nicer neighbourhoods of Sydney. Source: The Flying Vignale

  3. 1987

    Last inspection and insurance in Australia. Shortly afterwards the engine will not start any more and the car stays under a tarpaulin on the driveway by the pool. Source: The Flying Vignale

  4. August 1992

    Martin, then a student travelling through Australia, sees the little car under the tarpaulin when he drops a colleague off in Huntershill. He hears it has not run for six years and falls for it on the spot. Carole Grace turns out to have looked in vain for a buyer before. Source: The Flying Vignale

  5. September 1992

    Martin buys the Gamine. With a borrowed battery and some cleaning of the contact points the engine runs briefly under its own power. A stuck brake is knocked free so the car can roll. Source: The Flying Vignale

  6. 21 September 1992

    Instead of a sea container (A$1,580 plus port charges) the Gamine takes to the air by Qantas: A$2,625 all-in, and thanks to about 59 days shorter transit also much cheaper insurance. H.M. Towing Service takes the car to the Nippon Express warehouse for A$85. “The Flying Vignale.” Source: The Flying Vignale

  7. 23 September 1992

    Arrival at Schiphol; Nippon Express calls with the news. Import is not possible via Schiphol, only via Amsterdam and Rotterdam among others. Source: The Flying Vignale

  8. Late September 1992

    Customs clearance at the customs office on the Westzeedijk in Rotterdam, where the name Vignale Gamine is completely unknown. Deposit via KLM Cargo, transport by car ambulance (Wolders, Rotterdam) to Vlaardingen. Total import duty almost ƒ 1,500, including 90% age discount on the B.V.B. A photo is taken by a windmill in Vlaardingen — a sale condition set by the previous owner. Source: The Flying Vignale

  9. October 1992

    Brake system and headlamp reflectors renewed via Auto Europa Italia. The old reflectors were aimed for driving on the left (Australia / England). Source: The Flying Vignale

  10. 3 November 1992

    With a one-day registration and a weighbridge slip of 482 kg the car fails the RDW inspection on 8 points. Source: The Flying Vignale; RDW (weight 482 kg)

  11. 9 November 1992

    Six days later presented again and passed. The inspector first asks whether Martin really thinks he will get a registration for this, but becomes enthusiastic when it turns out it is not a home-build but a genuine Vignale Gamine. Source: The Flying Vignale

  12. 19 November 1992

    First registration in the Netherlands. The Gamine gets registration DH-30-93. The registration document initially still states that the vehicle is partly assembled from used parts — a mix-up of codes, corrected later. Source: RDW open data; The Flying Vignale

  13. Around 1994

    The car runs on DH-30-93 for well over a year. Trips with Yvon to the beach for a sunset, Chianti and pizza. The original plan to sell an Australian car in the Netherlands at a profit falls through for good. Source: The Flying Vignale

  14. 1997

    Participation in the Route des Vosges in France: 530 km each way. The Gamine is by far the smallest and slowest car in the field (18 hp, against Jaguars, Porsches and MGs). The mandatory stickers barely fit on the 25 cm high doors and end up on the rear wings. 3rd place in the “most desirable car” vote and a large trophy for “l’équipe le plus sympathique”. Source: Route des Vosges

  15. 2017

    “Nut/bolt” restoration. Soda-blasted, welded, sprayed in its original colour “Hollywood verde”.

  16. 27 December 2017

    Date of the current registration in the RDW register. Source: RDW open data

  17. 2020

    Last odometer reading recorded by the RDW. Source: RDW open data

  18. 29 August 2026

    Registration check: WAM-insured, MOT-exempt due to the 50-plus rule. The car is in all likelihood still on the road. Source: RDW open data

  19. 31 August 2026 — today

    Still in the Netherlands, last known owner Martin W. Turquoise, right-hand drive, chassis 1911571. The global register holds 38 photos of this car. Source: overview sheet
