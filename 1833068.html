What we know about this car

Chassis 110F1833068, built in 1968 and first registered in Italy that year. According to classic-car dealer Luzzago 1975 the car stayed with one family from new and passed from father to son, with matching numbers, the original first-registration booklet and the Italian black plates. The previous registration was the Italian MI G13537 (province of Milan).

That family made the car more comfortable and more usable, in its own words: Giannini competition wheels and a Giannini steering wheel, velvet seats and a sunroof. Drum brakes all round.

In 2022 and again in 2023 the car was for sale at Luzzago 1975 in Roncadelle near Brescia, for €21,000: white with a red interior and 15,145 km on the odometer. It then came to Germany and was given the registration BKS-FL-69 (Bernkastel-Wittlich district). In the register it is listed under the name of Andreas Weber.

Is the car still on the road?

No. The car was lost to a fire and sold as a wreck. A full rebuild is under way; the photo series continues into 2026. For Germany there is no public per-plate registration check.

The engine

The original matching-numbers 499cc Fiat 500 engine was lost in the fire. A rebuilt Fiat 126 engine is intended for the rebuild: an air-cooled twin with a custom-made stainless-steel twin-pipe sports exhaust. On the owner's YouTube channel the engine is test-run on an engine stand (15 March 2024) and, a little later, running installed in a red Fiat 500 (6 April 2024).

Distinguishing features

From before the fire, from the Luzzago photos: Giannini wheels, a Giannini steering wheel, red velvet seats, a chrome hoop behind the seats, twin wipers, a sunroof and chrome overriders. The register further mentions, among other things, a round mirror, a number plate between the bumper blades, a speedometer to 150 mph, extra gauges under the dashboard and a 12 V lighter. The dashboard and the interior were lost in the fire.
